## The Five-Step Loop in the Right Order

The official game description names the loop in five words: crunch, collect, unlock, upgrade, and expand. Each of those five words corresponds to one of the four progression tracks plus the collection step, and running them in the wrong order is the single biggest beginner mistake. Below is the verified order in which the loop should run during your first session, and each step links out to a deeper guide that covers the decision framework in detail.

| Step | Action | What It Produces | Time to Reach This Step |
|------|--------|------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Join a server and crunch the starter Salted Butter | First cash drops from cracked shells | Immediate (0–2 min) |
| 2 | Walk over the dropped cash to collect it | Banked cash ready to spend | Immediate (0–3 min) |
| 3 | Buy the next butter flavor when the prompt allows | Multiplier on every future crunch | First 5–10 minutes |
| 4 | Buy the first roller upgrade when cash allows | Higher throughput so the new butter is actually crunched | First 10–20 minutes |
| 5 | Expand the butter floor once the roller covers it | Both active and offline income rise | First 20–30 minutes |

The collection step is where many new players lose time, because dropped cash sits on the floor until collected and the idle timer does not credit you for cash you have not picked up. If you walk away from the keyboard without sweeping your floor, you are leaving compounding income on the table. Make the collection sweep a habit on every login. It is free income that you have already earned but not yet banked.

## What to Buy First in Crunch My Butter

Better butter almost always wins the "what should I buy first" question. EX10 describes the loop as "unlock better butter", which makes flavor tier the primary multiplier on cash per crunch. A new butter that pays roughly twice what your current flavor pays will double your effective income from every subsequent roller pass, which dwarfs the throughput gain from a single roller upgrade in the same price band. In practice, this means you should buy the next butter flavor as soon as the in-game prompt allows it, then buy the roller upgrade once your new flavor is generating cash faster than the roller can sweep it.

This decision flips depending on your current state. If your roller is sweeping the whole floor between respawns and you still have cash on the floor, the roller is the bottleneck and you should upgrade it before buying the next butter. If your roller is constantly moving but the floor stays mostly empty, the butter is the bottleneck and you should save for the next flavor. Watching your floor for thirty seconds tells you which side of the equation is starving.

| Current State | Buy This Next | Why |
|--------------|--------------|-----|
| Floor is full of cracked butter the roller is not reaching | Roller upgrade | Roller throughput is the limiting factor |
| Roller sweeps the floor between every respawn | Next butter flavor | Butter income is the limiting factor |
| Both butter and roller are keeping up with each other | Area expansion | Floor size becomes the new ceiling |
| You have a large unspent cash balance | Whatever unlocks next | Idle minutes spent sitting on cash are compounding minutes wasted |

Sitting on a large cash balance feels safe but it actually starves your compounding income. Every idle minute spent on unspent cash is income you are not earning, because the offline layer multiplies your current butter, roller, and floor size rather than your cash in the bank. A player who buys the next unlock immediately and lets the offline layer run on the larger economy is always ahead of a player who is saving up for a "big" purchase that never gets any better the longer they wait.

## Crunching and Collection Mechanics

Crunching is the act of pressing a butter with your roller until its outer shell cracks, which causes the butter to drop cash and disappear. Each butter has a crunchy outer shell that cracks audibly under the roller, and that crack is the signature ASMR sound of the game. The squishy physics tag means butter deforms under pressure rather than simply despawning, which is part of why the visuals feel satisfying even before any optimization happens. Creamy golden butter against the cobalt blue background is the loop the developers leaned into, and the game has been classified by Roblox under Simulation in the Incremental Simulator subcategory.

The roller moves with continuous input on PC and with the on-screen joystick on touch devices, and the contact between the roller and the butter is what triggers the cash drop. On mobile, lower Roblox graphics quality if butter physics stutter because the crunch payout depends on the roller actually contacting butter, not just on it appearing to pass over. Servers cap at 8 players so even on older phones performance stays stable, and there is no voice chat or camera so no extra permissions are needed.

Dropped cash sits on the floor until collected. You can collect by walking over it, and the auto-collection radius expands with certain upgrades that the live game shows in the upgrade menu. Until you have that auto-collection upgrade, sweeping your own area is free income you have already earned. Make the sweep a habit on every login and you will accumulate more cash in the same session without grinding any harder.

## The Four-Track Progression Explained

The four progression tracks are butter flavor unlocks, roller upgrades, area expansion, and offline accumulation. Each track has its own scaling curve and its own ceiling, and the tracks interact multiplicatively rather than additively. Buying a more expensive butter flavor before upgrading your roller means the new flavor's higher cash per crunch is bottlenecked by the slow roller, so your effective income barely moves. Expanding your floor before you have a roller to cover it wastes both money and idle income because the extra butter sits uncrunched.

The mechanics favor patience and reinvestment over hoarding. A player who sits on a large cash balance is leaving compounding income on the table, and a player who buys an upgrade they cannot yet afford to use is wasting the same compounding. Understanding these interactions is the only skill the game actually tests. ## Newcomer Offline Layer

EX10's tagline is "earn cash while you sleep", which confirms that an idle layer exists even if its exact rates and caps are not published. The way to maximize the offline layer is to leave the game in a state where you have just bought an upgrade and your butter floor is fully covered by your roller. That combination maximizes the cash you accumulate while you are away from the keyboard. If you leave the game with unspent cash sitting in your balance, the offline layer does not compound that cash into more cash; it compounds the size of your butter, roller, and floor into cash. Spend first, then walk away.

Most players report 20 to 40 minute sessions as the sweet spot before the diminishing returns of the current butter tier kick in. Because the offline layer keeps accumulating, leaving after a strong session and returning later is often more efficient than grinding the same tier past its breakpoint. The first time you log in after a long break you should see a stack of uncollected cash on the floor and an even bigger buyable unlock waiting for you. That is the compounding loop working as designed.

The session start order matters. Collect your offline cash first, buy the next unlock, then start crunching. If you start crunching before collecting, the new cash drops mix with the old pile and the auto-collection will pick both up at once, but the buyable unlock you could have afforded earlier in the session is delayed by however long it took you to sweep the floor. Order is compounding.

## Newcomer Errors

The biggest beginner mistake is treating the roller and butter as independent rather than multiplicative. A player who buys every butter flavor in order without ever upgrading the roller will see their effective income stall because the slow roller cannot crunch the new, more expensive butter at a rate that justifies its price. The opposite mistake is buying every roller upgrade in order without ever unlocking a new butter, which starves the roller of richer income and wastes the throughput gain on the same low-paying flavor. The loop only compounds when you upgrade whichever side is currently the bottleneck, not when you upgrade whichever side you happen to have enough cash for.

Another common mistake is ignoring the collection sweep. If you walk away from the keyboard without sweeping your floor, the offline timer credits you only for the cash that has already been banked. Walking back to your computer to find a pile of untouched cash means you missed out on the same compounding you would have earned by simply walking across the floor before stepping away. Build the sweep into your logout routine.

A third mistake is hoarding cash. Some players hold a large balance because the next unlock feels expensive, but every idle minute spent on unspent cash is compounding income wasted. The game is designed around reinvestment, not saving. If you cannot afford the next unlock yet, you are not saving for it; you are losing the offline income that an earlier, cheaper unlock would have produced. Buy the smaller upgrade today and let it compound, then come back to the bigger one when the smaller one has paid for it.
