## The Reinvestment Discipline

The reinvestment discipline is the foundation of the fastest progression. Every cash balance should be reinvested into the next unlock as soon as it is affordable. Holding cash in the balance is the most expensive mistake a player can make, because the AFK layer does not multiply unspent cash into more cash. The player who reinvests immediately lets the AFK layer work on the larger economy; the player who hoards lets the AFK layer work on the smaller economy.

The cadence is simple: check the next unlock price after every crunch session, and buy as soon as the price is affordable. The check takes a few seconds, and the buy takes another few seconds. The rest of the time should be spent crunching, not browsing the menu. The first hour should be at least 80 percent crunching and 20 percent upgrade decisions.

The reinvestment discipline is especially important in the early game because the compounding is fastest in the early game. A player who reinvests every cash balance in the first hour will be a compounding cycle or two ahead of a player who hoards for the first hour. The compounding gap grows with every idle minute, which is why the discipline matters most in the early game.

## The Active Session Routine

The active session routine is the second key to the fastest progression. The routine has three components: a continuous crunch rhythm, a regular collection sweep, and a buy decision after every crunch session. The routine should be repeatable, which means the player should be able to execute it for 20–40 minutes without fatigue.

The crunch rhythm is a continuous sweep across the butter floor in a figure-eight or oval pattern. The roller should be moving every moment you are actively playing, and the direction should reverse before the roller reaches the edge of the floor. A roller that sits idle at the edge of the floor is leaving cash on the table because no butter is being crunched.

The collection sweep should happen after every crunch session and before every logout. The sweep takes ten to thirty seconds depending on the floor size, and the compounding gain from sweeping is significant. Every minute you leave unbanked cash on the floor is a minute the AFK layer is not crediting your banked balance.

The buy decision should happen as soon as the next unlock is affordable. Open the upgrade menu, check the price of the next butter flavor or roller upgrade, close the menu, crunch until the price is affordable, then reopen the menu and buy. The decision should take less than thirty seconds, and the rest of the time should be spent crunching.

## The Session Length Sweet Spot

The session length sweet spot is 20–40 minutes of active play followed by AFK accumulation. Sessions shorter than 20 minutes do not give the compounding enough time to drive the next purchase, and sessions longer than 40 minutes hit the diminishing returns of the current butter tier. The 20–40 minute window is the sweet spot because it balances active reinvestment against AFK compounding.

Within the session, the rhythm should be continuous crunch for the first 15 minutes, then a buy decision, then continuous crunch for the next 15 minutes, then another buy decision. Two buy decisions per 40-minute session is the maximum the current butter tier can sustain, and the second buy is usually the next butter flavor or the next roller upgrade.

Between sessions, the AFK layer runs while the player is away. The AFK layer multiplies the player's current configuration, so the compounding is meaningful even when the player is not at the keyboard. The right balance is to make the next purchase before logging out so the AFK layer can work on the larger economy.

## The Overnight Compounding Cycle

The overnight compounding cycle is the largest single-day accumulation in the game. The cycle has three components: an evening logout with a balanced configuration, six to eight hours of AFK accumulation, and a morning login with the next purchase. Two compounding cycles in one sleep.

The evening logout should be in the balanced configuration: the roller covers the floor, the banked balance is as high as possible, and the next unlock is bought or queued. The AFK layer multiplies the balanced configuration while the player sleeps.

The morning login should sweep the floor, bank the AFK accumulation, and buy the next unlock now made affordable by the overnight compounding. The cycle then repeats: evening logout with the new balanced configuration, overnight AFK accumulation, morning login with the next purchase.

The overnight cycle compounds over time. The first night might produce a small accumulation, but the second night produces a larger accumulation because the AFK layer is working on the larger economy. The third night is larger still, and so on. The compounding compounds, which is why the overnight cycle is the largest single-day accumulation.

## The Bottleneck Recognition

The bottleneck recognition is the third key to the fastest progression. The player who recognizes the bottleneck and invests in the track that resolves it will out-compound the player who invests in arbitrary tracks. The recognition is simple: watch the floor for thirty seconds and identify the side that is starving.

If the floor is full of uncrunched butter, the roller is the bottleneck. The roller is not crunching the butter fast enough, and the next investment should be the next roller upgrade. The compounding gain is the throughput on the rich butter, which is the largest possible marginal gain.

If the roller is sweeping the floor between respawns and there are still cracked butters on the ground, the collection radius is the bottleneck. The next investment should be the next butter flavor with a wider drop radius or the next area expansion. The compounding gain is the cash on the floor that the player was leaving behind.

If the roller is sweeping the floor and there are no unbanked cash drops, the butter is the bottleneck. The next investment should be the next butter flavor. The compounding gain is the higher cash per crunch on the new butter.

## The Roller Coverage Test

The roller coverage test is a simple check that determines whether the player should buy the area expansion. The test is: if the roller is sweeping the floor between respawns and there are no uncrunched butters, the roller has spare capacity and the area expansion is justified. If the roller is constantly moving but the floor stays mostly empty, the roller is the bottleneck and the area expansion is not justified.

The test takes thirty seconds and should be done after every major investment. The compounding gain from buying the area expansion when the roller is ready is significant; the compounding loss from buying the area expansion when the roller is not ready is also significant. The test prevents both the gain and the loss.

The test is qualitative because the exact roller coverage is not published. The principle is that the roller should be able to keep up with the butter respawn rate, and the floor should be empty of uncrunched butter when the roller is sweeping. The test is the player's check against over-investment in the wrong track.

## Common Fast-Progression Mistakes

The most common fast-progression mistake is over-investing in one track. The four tracks interact multiplicatively, and the compounding is fastest when the player invests in the track that is currently the bottleneck. A player who keeps buying butter flavors after the roller is bottlenecked is wasting the compounding.

The second most common mistake is skipping the collection sweep. The AFK timer credits you only for the cash that has already been banked. Walking away from the keyboard without sweeping your floor means you missed out on the same compounding you would have earned by simply walking across the floor before stepping away.

The right comparison is the active income during a session plus the AFK income during the absence.
