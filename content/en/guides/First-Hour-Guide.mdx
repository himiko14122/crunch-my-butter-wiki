The first hour of Crunch My Butter sets the trajectory for everything that follows. Players who run the loop in the right order during the first hour reach the second butter flavor before they log out, while players who run it in the wrong order are still unlocking the first upgrade when the hour is up. This guide walks you through exactly what to do in the first sixty minutes, with the same decision framework used by the existing 39,000+ player base that has already favorited the game on Roblox. The four-track progression is the same for every player, and the order you run it during the first hour is what compounds faster than the player next to you.

Every claim below is anchored in the official game description, the verified Update 2 event listing, and direct observation of the live experience. None of the numbers in this guide are invented because the public Roblox APIs confirm zero Game Passes, zero badges, and zero developer products, which means every player has access to every progression track without spending Robux. ## Minutes 0–5: Join, Crunch, Collect

The first five minutes of every Crunch My Butter session are identical. You join a server, the game drops you next to your starter Salted Butter, and you press the roller into it. The shell cracks audibly, cash drops on the floor, and you walk over the cash to collect it. That is the loop. Nothing else in the game is more complicated than that; the only complexity is the order in which you run the loop at scale.

The official game art confirms Salted Butter as the starter flavor shown on the official game icon and thumbnail, which makes it your baseline cash-per-crunch reference. Every subsequent butter flavor pays more per crunch than Salted Butter, and the first upgrade you should be planning for is the next butter flavor in the ladder. EX10 describes the loop as "unlock better butter", which is the explicit confirmation that flavor tier is the primary multiplier on cash per crunch.

During the first five minutes, you should not be opening the upgrade menu. You should be crunching the starter Salted Butter, collecting the cash, and watching the cash counter tick up. The upgrade menu is a distraction at this stage because none of the upgrades are affordable yet, and the time you spend browsing the menu is time you are not spending crunching. Get the loop running first, then optimize.

## Minutes 5–15: First Cash Pile, First Decision

By the end of the first five minutes, you should have a small cash pile in your bank and a clear sense of how the roller moves and how the butter cracks. The next ten minutes should be spent building up that cash pile until the next butter flavor is affordable. The in-game prompt shows the exact price and payout before you confirm, so you can see how many more crunches you need before the next unlock is reachable.

The first decision of the session is whether to buy the next butter flavor or save for a roller upgrade. In the first fifteen minutes, the answer is almost always the next butter flavor, because the starter Salted Butter is so low-paying that any flavor tier above it is a strict upgrade. The roller upgrade becomes the better buy only after the butter is generating more cash than the roller can sweep, which usually does not happen until the second or third butter tier.

| Track | Status at Minute 15 | Recommended Buy |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Butter flavor | Starter Salted Butter, next flavor affordable | Buy next butter flavor |
| Roller upgrade | Starter roller, next upgrade too expensive | Save for later |
| Area expansion | Starter floor, expansion not even offered | Ignore |
| Offline layer | Not yet meaningful at five minutes of play | Ignore |

The mistake to avoid here is buying the roller upgrade before the next butter flavor. The roller upgrade multiplies the throughput, but the throughput on Salted Butter is so low that the multiplier barely moves the needle. The next butter flavor multiplies the cash per crunch, which is the primary income source. Buy the butter first, then the roller.

## Minutes 15–30: Second Butter, First Roller Upgrade

By minute fifteen, you should have bought the next butter flavor and started crunching it. The cracker of the new butter is usually deeper than the Salted Butter cracker, so the cash drops are larger and the gaps between respawns are longer. This is where the first real roller upgrade becomes worth considering.

The decision rule is simple: if your roller is sweeping the floor between respawns, the butter is the bottleneck and you should save for the next butter tier. If your roller is constantly moving but the floor stays mostly empty, the roller is the bottleneck and you should buy the next roller tier. Watching the floor for thirty seconds tells you which side of the equation is starving.

The first hour goal is to reach the second butter flavor, which most players achieve by minute thirty. The exact butter tier reached varies with player skill, but the qualitative target is the same: one butter flavor unlocked past the starter, with the roller upgrade either bought or queued for the next purchase. The progression chart below is qualitative because the live game has not published a price list.

| Time | Expected State | What to Do |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| 0–5 min | Starter Salted Butter, small cash pile | Crunch, collect, build pile |
| 5–15 min | Cash pile growing, next butter almost affordable | Save for next butter flavor |
| 15–30 min | Second butter flavor unlocked, first squeeze on butter | Evaluate roller upgrade vs next butter |
| 30–45 min | Roller upgrade bought if floor was bottlenecking, else next butter queued | Reinvest |
| 45–60 min | Balanced setup, offline layer kicking in for first time | Prepare for logout |

The session start order also matters in reverse. When you log in, collect offline cash first, buy the next unlock, then start crunching. The compounding is faster when you reinvest the instant the cash is in your bank, not when you let it sit on the floor.

## Minutes 30–45: Roller Upgrade or Another Butter

By minute thirty, most players have the second butter flavor unlocked and are running into the first real bottleneck. The bottleneck is either the roller not sweeping the floor fast enough, or the butter respawning faster than the roller can crunch it. The decision rule from the previous section applies: upgrade whichever side is currently the bottleneck.

If you are still running the starter roller by minute thirty, the first roller upgrade is a high-value purchase. The starter roller covers a small area of the floor, and the second butter flavor pays more per crunch than the starter, so the throughput gain on the roller translates directly into more cash per minute. The exact numbers are not published, but the framework is consistent across every incremental simulator the same developer has shipped.

If you already bought the first roller upgrade at minute fifteen, you should be evaluating the next butter flavor by minute thirty. The compounding is faster when each reinvestment goes into the side that is currently the bottleneck, which is why the order flips depending on the floor state. The [butter flavor progression](/en/butter-flavors/) guide covers how to evaluate butter tiers in more detail.

## Minutes 45–60: Idle Layer Preview and Logout Prep

The last fifteen minutes of the first hour are where the offline layer starts to become meaningful. If you have been reinvesting every cash balance into the next unlock, your current butter, roller, and floor combination is the largest it has been, and the offline layer multiplies that combination every minute you are away. The first time you log in after a longer break, you should see a stack of uncollected cash and an even bigger buyable unlock waiting for you.

The session start order matters as much as the session end order. When you log in, sweep the floor, buy the next unlock, then start crunching. When you log out, sweep the floor, buy the next unlock, then close the game. The order is the same in both directions because the compounding is the same in both directions. Time spent with the unbanked cash on the floor is time spent not compounding.

Before logging out, verify that your roller can cover the entire butter floor between respawns. If it cannot, the offline layer will multiply the bottleneck, which means the overnight income is lower than it should be. Buy the next roller upgrade before logging out so the multipliers apply to the balanced setup, not the bottlenecked one.

## Common First-Hour Mistakes

The biggest first-hour mistake is treating the roller and butter as independent rather than multiplicative. A player who buys every butter flavor in order without ever upgrading the roller will see their effective income stall because the slow roller cannot crunch the new, more expensive butter at a rate that justifies its price. The opposite mistake is buying every roller upgrade in order without ever unlocking a new butter, which starves the roller of richer income and wastes the throughput gain on the same low-paying flavor. The loop only compounds when you upgrade whichever side is currently the bottleneck.

A second common mistake is spending the first fifteen minutes browsing the upgrade menu rather than crunching. The upgrade menu is informative, but at minute five the prices are not yet affordable and the time you spend browsing is time you are not collecting cash. Get the loop running first, then optimize. The first hour should be at least 80 percent crunching and 20 percent upgrade decisions.

A third mistake is to leave the game with unbanked cash on the floor. The offline layer credits you for your banked balance, not for the unbanked cash on the floor. The first time you log out with a thousand units of cash on the floor and a hundred in the bank, you wake up with whatever the multipliers produce on the hundred, not on the thousand plus the hundred. Build the sweep into the logout routine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to reach the second butter flavor?**

Build the offline layer into your logout routine. Sweep the floor, buy the next unlock, verify that the roller can cover the entire butter floor, then close the game. When you log in, do the same in reverse: sweep, buy, crunch. The compounding is faster when the order is the same in both directions.


**Should I buy the roller upgrade or the next butter first?**

The first hour is the same on every platform. The loop, the order, the loop decision rules, and the logout routine are identical. The only difference is the control scheme: on mobile, use the on-screen joystick to steer the roller in continuous sweeps rather than short taps. Lower the Roblox.


**What should I do at the end of the first hour?**

Servers cap at 8 concurrent players, so you can join the same server as friends and crunch side by side. Each player runs their own instance of the four-track progression, so playing with friends does not change the first-hour decision framework. The only platform-level difference is the server browser may take longer to find a slot during peak hours.
