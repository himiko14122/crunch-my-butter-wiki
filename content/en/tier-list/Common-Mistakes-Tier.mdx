The mistakes are organized from the most expensive to the least expensive, in terms of compounding income lost. The biggest mistake (hoarding cash) is the one that costs the most compounding because the AFK layer runs every minute the player is online with unbanked cash sitting in the balance.

## Mistake 1: Hoarding Cash

The most expensive mistake is hoarding cash. Some players hold a large balance because the next unlock feels expensive, but every idle minute spent on unspent cash is compounding income wasted. The game is designed around reinvestment, not saving. The principle is the same regardless of the player's current butter flavor or roller tier, as confirmed by the live API snapshot from July 26, 2026.

The cure is to buy the smaller upgrade today and let it compound, then come back to the bigger one when the smaller one has paid for it. The compounding is always larger when you reinvest immediately and let the AFK layer work on the reinvested economy, not when you hoard and let the AFK layer work on a smaller economy.

The mistake is especially costly in the early game because the AFK layer multiplies the smaller economy by a smaller number. A player who hoards for thirty minutes loses a full compounding cycle that a reinvesting player gets for free.

## Mistake 2: Ignoring the Collection Sweep

The second most expensive mistake is ignoring the collection sweep. If you walk away from the keyboard without sweeping your floor, the AFK timer credits you only for the cash that has already been banked. The compounding effect is meaningful and worth prioritizing during every active session according to the four-track progression framework documented on the wiki.

The cure is to build the sweep into your logout routine. The sweep takes ten to thirty seconds depending on the floor size, and the compounding gain from sweeping is significant. Every minute you leave unbanked cash on the floor is a minute the AFK layer is not crediting your banked balance.

The mistake is especially costly overnight because the AFK layer runs for six to eight hours while you sleep. A player who logs out with a thousand units of cash on the floor and a hundred in the bank wakes up with whatever the multipliers produce on the hundred, not on the thousand plus the hundred.

## Mistake 3: Buying the Wrong Upgrade First

The third most expensive mistake is buying the wrong upgrade first. The two most common wrong-upgrade scenarios are buying the roller upgrade before the next butter flavor, and buying the area expansion before the roller is ready to cover it. Both scenarios waste compounding because the next investment cannot be fully utilized.

The cure is to follow the tier list priority. The default priority is butter flavor (S) > roller upgrade (A) > area expansion (B). The priority flips when the roller is bottlenecking the butter, in which case the roller upgrade is the higher priority.

The mistake is especially costly in the early game because the early investments set the trajectory for the entire session. A player who buys the roller upgrade before the next butter flavor will see their effective income stall because the slow roller cannot crunch the new, more expensive butter at a rate that justifies its price.

## Mistake 4: Over-Investing in One Track

The fourth most expensive mistake is over-investing in one tier. The four tiers interact multiplicatively, and the compounding is fastest when the player invests in the tier that is currently the bottleneck. A player who keeps buying butter flavors after the roller is bottlenecked is wasting the compounding because the slow roller cannot crunch the new butter at a rate that justifies its price.

The cure is to recognize the bottleneck and invest in the tier that resolves it. The bottleneck changes as the configuration changes, so the player who resolves the bottleneck and then keeps investing in the same tier is wasting the compounding.

The mistake is especially costly in the late game because the marginal value of any single investment is smaller than the marginal value of the same investment during the early game. The late-game player who keeps investing in the wrong tier is not just losing the compounding, they are losing the opportunity cost of the right-tier investment.

## Mistake 5: Buying the Area Expansion Too Early

The fifth most expensive mistake is buying the area expansion before the roller is ready to cover it. A larger floor that the roller cannot cover wastes both the cash spent on the expansion and the idle income the new floor would have generated.

The cure is to wait for the roller to cover the current floor consistently before expanding. The roller coverage test is simple: if the roller is sweeping the floor between respawns and there are no uncrunched butters, the roller has spare capacity and the area expansion is justified.

The mistake is especially costly because the area expansion is the most expensive upgrade in the early game, and the wasted compounding is proportional to the cost. A player who buys the area expansion before the roller is ready will not see any return on the expansion until they upgrade the roller.

## Mistake 6: Browsing the Upgrade Menu Too Long

The sixth most expensive mistake is browsing the upgrade menu too long. The upgrade menu is informative, but during the first fifteen minutes of a session, the prices are not yet affordable and the time you spend browsing is time you are not collecting cash. The first hour should be at least 80 percent crunching and 20 percent upgrade decisions.

The cure is to commit to a quick decision and execute it. Open the upgrade menu, check the price of the next butter flavor, close the menu, crunch until the price is affordable, then reopen the menu and buy. The decision should take less than thirty seconds.

The mistake is not very costly in isolation, but it compounds over multiple sessions. A player who spends ten extra minutes browsing per session will lose ten minutes of compounding per session, which adds up to hours of compounding over a month.

## Mistake 7: Leaving the Game with a Bottlenecked Setup

The seventh most expensive mistake is leaving the game with a bottlenecked setup. The AFK layer multiplies the current configuration, so a bottlenecked configuration produces a smaller overnight accumulation. The compounding is only meaningful when the player balances the four tracks before logging out.

The cure is to verify the configuration before logging out. The verification is simple: check that the roller can cover the floor, check that the banked balance is as high as possible, check that the next unlock is bought or queued.

The mistake is especially costly overnight because the AFK layer runs for six to eight hours. A player who logs out with a bottlenecked configuration wakes up to a smaller accumulation than a player who logs out with a balanced configuration.

## Mistake 8: Comparing Active Income to AFK Income Unfairly

The eighth most expensive mistake is comparing active income to AFK income unfairly. The active rate is slightly higher than the AFK rate for the same multiplier configuration, but the AFK rate runs while the player is away, which makes the comparison misleading.

The cure is to use the AFK layer as a designed part of the economy rather than as a competitor to active play. The active income during the session is the compounding that drives the next purchase, and the AFK income during the absence is the compounding that drives the next purchase after the return.

The mistake is not very costly in isolation, but it can lead to over-active play, which causes player burnout and a less enjoyable experience. The right balance is 20–40 minute active sessions followed by AFK accumulation. The pattern is consistent with what the wiki tracks in the live game snapshot and what the developer confirms through the official Roblox game page.

## The Cumulative Effect

The cumulative effect of these mistakes is that a player who makes two or three of them will be a compounding cycle or two behind a player who makes none of them. The compounding cycle is roughly 20–40 minutes during active play and six to eight hours overnight, so a two-cycle gap is the difference between the right-track trajectory and the wrong-track trajectory over a few hours.

The cure is to recognize the mistakes early and correct them before they compound. The recognition is simple: if you find yourself with unbanked cash on the floor, sweep it. If you find yourself with a bottlenecked roller, upgrade the roller. If you find yourself browsing the upgrade menu for too long, close it and crunch.

The long-term trajectory is determined by the small decisions you make every session. The decisions add up to the compounding gap, and the compounding gap is the difference between a player who reaches the late butter tiers in a week and a player who reaches them in a month.
